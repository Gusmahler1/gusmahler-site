## Market Integrity

### CFTC and DOJ charge soldier with insider trading on Maduro contract

On April 23, the CFTC filed a civil complaint in SDNY and the DOJ announced parallel criminal charges against Gannon Ken Van Dyke, an Army soldier who used classified knowledge of a covert Venezuela operation to purchase 436,000+ "Yes" shares on Polymarket's "Maduro Out by January 31, 2026?" contract between December 30 and January 2. Trading under the handle "Burdensome-Mix," he made approximately $404,000 when the contract resolved. The CFTC charged commodities fraud, wire fraud, and unlawful use of nonpublic government information. Van Dyke was released pending proceedings.

Polymarket confirmed it cooperated with investigators. Kalshi stated it had independently identified and blocked Van Dyke before the charges were filed.

### Kalshi suspends candidates; platforms expand conflict-of-interest rules

Kalshi fined three federal candidates amounts ranging from $539 to $6,200 and imposed five-year trading suspensions for trading on markets tied to their own races. Both Kalshi and Polymarket subsequently announced expanded policies: politicians barred from trading on their own races, athletes from their own leagues, and employees from contracts tied to their employers. These moves preempt anticipated regulatory guidance.

<ImplicationBox items={["Voluntary enforcement and policy expansion ahead of CFTC rulemaking gives platforms a credibility argument against prescriptive federal rules on integrity — they're already policing themselves.", "Expanding restrictions to employees and athletes signals platforms anticipate regulators will target broader misuse-of-position trades beyond political candidates."]} />

## Company News

### Both platforms launch perpetual futures as CFTC-registered DCMs

Polymarket opened a waitlist for perpetual futures contracts on crypto and stocks with up to 10x leverage and no expiration date. Kalshi announced a parallel product, codenamed "Timeless," with launch scheduled for April 27 in New York. Both platforms' CFTC Designated Contract Market status provides a domestic regulatory advantage over offshore derivatives exchanges. Combined year-to-date trading volume stands at approximately $67 billion, with Kalshi at $37B and Polymarket at $30B.

<ImplicationBox items={["Perpetual futures diversify revenue away from sports and political contracts — the categories under the most legal and reputational pressure.", "DCM registration becomes a competitive moat if regulators move to restrict non-registered platforms from offering leveraged derivatives to U.S. retail customers."]} />

### Polymarket in talks to raise $400M at a $15B valuation

Polymarket is in discussions to raise $400M at a $15B valuation — a 66% increase from its previous $9B ICE-backed round, but roughly one-third below Kalshi's $22B. The discount likely reflects Kalshi's dominant U.S. market share, longer fee history, and cleaner domestic regulatory profile. March 2026 was Polymarket's first $10B monthly volume month. Kalshi and Polymarket have announced data partnerships with CNBC, CNN, Fox News, AP, Substack, Dow Jones, and major sports leagues including MLB, MLS, UFC, and LaLiga.

<ImplicationBox items={["The valuation gap is a market signal on regulatory clarity — Kalshi's CFTC footprint commands a premium that Polymarket's crypto-native architecture doesn't yet match.", "Media and sports integrations accelerate mainstream legitimization, which matters for the ANPRM outcome: the more prediction market prices appear in broadcast, the harder it is for regulators to frame them as fringe gambling products."]} />

## International

### Brazil bans non-economic event contracts, blocks 27 platforms

On April 24, Brazil's National Monetary Council passed Resolution 5,298, banning derivative contracts tied to non-economic events including sports, elections, and cultural outcomes. Telecom regulator Anatel was directed to block 27 platforms, including Kalshi and Polymarket. Finance Minister Dario Durigan cited investor protection; Economic Reforms Secretary Regis Dudena characterized the products as "financial instruments that resemble betting in practice."
