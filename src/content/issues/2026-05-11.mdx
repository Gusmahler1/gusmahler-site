## Federal Courts

### Arizona federal court blocks state enforcement against Kalshi

Judge Michael T. Liburdi of the District of Arizona granted Kalshi a preliminary injunction on May 5, blocking Arizona regulators and prosecutors from pursuing 20 criminal and civil charges against the platform for offering sports and event contracts without a state gaming license. The 17-page order rests on the same statutory theory the CFTC has pressed in its own preemption suits: that the Commodity Exchange Act grants the agency exclusive jurisdiction over DCM-registered event contracts, displacing state gaming law. CFTC Chair Selig publicly applauded the ruling after it was issued. ([Covers, May 6](https://www.covers.com/industry/judges-ruling-keeps-arizona-from-taking-legal-action-against-kalshi-may-6-2026) · [Casino.org, May 6](https://www.casino.org/news/federal-judge-blocks-arizona-criminal-case-against-kalshi/))

<ImplicationBox items={["A preliminary injunction requires showing likelihood of success on the merits. Judge Liburdi's willingness to grant it on preemption grounds is the strongest district-court signal yet that the CEA exclusivity argument has real traction.", "CFTC Chair Selig's public response to a ruling in a private party's case is unusual for the agency. It signals the CFTC intends to treat favorable district rulings as validation for its parallel preemption suits against Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, and New York.", "The 20-charge scope of the blocked enforcement illustrates the exposure platforms face in states with active gaming regulators. A favorable Ninth Circuit ruling in the pending Nevada case would eliminate that exposure across the western circuits."]} />

### Washington federal court remands state suit back to state court

Judge John C. Coughenour of the Western District of Washington reached the opposite conclusion on May 5, ordering Kalshi's case back to state court after finding that Washington's gambling enforcement lawsuit doesn't belong in federal court. Coughenour rejected Kalshi's removal argument and concluded gambling regulation is a state prerogative that the CEA doesn't supersede. Kalshi moved on May 8 to stay the remand order pending appeal to the Ninth Circuit, arguing that being forced to litigate in state court while the appeal is pending "would render Kalshi's appellate rights meaningless." ([ReadWrite, May 8](https://readwrite.com/kalshi-seeks-federal-pause-washington-gambling-lawsuit/) · [Gambling Insider, May 9](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/159623/ninth-circuit-tribal-request-kalshi-stay-washington-prediction-market-case) · [Law360, May 8](https://www.law360.com/capitalmarkets/articles/2472982))

<ImplicationBox items={["Arizona and Washington handed down opposite outcomes the same day, two district judges applying the same federal statute. That divergence is the condition that gives circuit courts review jurisdiction and motivates Supreme Court petitions.", "Remand to state court is a procedural ruling, not a merits loss. But litigating in a state court simultaneously hostile to preemption is a meaningful operational risk while the appeal works through the Ninth Circuit.", "Kalshi's stay motion frames the appellate-rights argument carefully: if the Ninth Circuit agrees, the Washington case stays federal during appeal; if denied, Kalshi faces parallel state and federal litigation tracks at once."]} />

### Ninth Circuit denies California tribes' consolidation request

The Ninth Circuit on May 6 denied a request from three California tribes — the Blue Lake Rancheria, the Chicken Ranch Rancheria of Me-Wuk Indians, and the Picayune Rancheria of the Chukchansi Indians — to assign their Kalshi appeal to the same panel handling the consolidated Nevada case. The denial keeps the California and Nevada tracks on separate panels, which means the circuit could issue conflicting analyses of the same preemption question before either case reaches en banc review. ([SCCG Management, May 13](https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/05/13/ninth-circuit-denial-fragments-west-coast-cea-challenges/) · [Slotsup, May 9](https://www.slotsup.com/news/ninth-circuit-rejects-tribal-request-as-kalshi))

<ImplicationBox items={["Fragmented panels raise the risk of an intracircuit split — two different outcomes on the same statutory question within the same circuit. That outcome would accelerate en banc petition and sharpen Supreme Court interest.", "The consolidation denial is a procedural setback for the tribes, who had strategic reasons to want their case heard alongside the Nevada platform suit. Separate tracks mean separate briefing timelines and a less coordinated argument.", "The Ninth Circuit's Nevada oral arguments were April 16. Any Nevada ruling won't bind the California panel — two bites at the same circuit, potentially producing two different answers on the same preemption question."]} />

## Company News

### Kalshi closes $1 billion Series F at $22 billion valuation

Kalshi closed a $1 billion Series F on May 7 at a $22 billion post-money valuation, led by Coatue and joined by Sequoia Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, IVP, Paradigm, Morgan Stanley, and ARK Invest. The round doubles Kalshi's valuation from its prior mark roughly five months ago. The company's annualized trading volume has tripled in six months to approximately $178 billion; it holds roughly 90% of U.S. prediction market activity. ([TechCrunch, May 7](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/kalshi-doubles-valuation-in-5-months-hitting-22-billion/) · [Crowdfund Insider, May 7](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/05/277962-prediction-markets-kalshi-hits-22b-valuation-in-latest-funding-round/))

### DraftKings and FanDuel signal deeper prediction-market push

DraftKings disclosed on its Q1 earnings call that it plans to spend approximately $300 million in 2026 primarily on marketing its prediction-market products, describing them as among its "fastest to profitability" lines. FanDuel's parent company Flutter separately disclosed it has begun market-making for a "major third-party prediction-market platform." DraftKings launched a "Combos" multi-leg event-contract product on May 11. ## Congressional & Legislative Activity

### House members push to mirror Senate's prediction-market trading ban

Rep. Ashley Hinson (R-IA) introduced a House rule resolution on May 7 to extend the Senate's prediction-market trading prohibition to House members and staff. On the same day, Reps. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) and Michael Cloud (R-TX) wrote Speaker Mike Johnson and Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries calling for parallel rules. Neither resolution has been scheduled for a floor vote. Following last week's unanimous Senate adoption of S. Res. 708, the House response has so far produced competing proposals rather than a unified measure. ([Bloomberg, May 7](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-07/prediction-market-ban-for-us-house-members-staff-gets-gop-push) · [Roll Call, May 7](https://rollcall.com/2026/05/07/senators-banned-themselves-from-prediction-markets-will-house/))

<ImplicationBox items={["Competing proposals without a scheduled vote is the typical pattern when a chamber agrees on the political optics of a ban but lacks agreement on scope. The Senate moved by unanimous consent in under 24 hours; the House shows no equivalent urgency.", "A House rule change requires a majority floor vote, unlike the Senate's unanimous-consent resolution. That's a higher procedural bar, which explains why competing draft proposals are circulating rather than a unified bill moving to markup."]} />

## Market Integrity

### Campaign staffers report profiting from trades tied to their own candidates

NPR reported on May 7 that campaign staffers on active federal races said they had made thousands of dollars trading on prediction markets tied to their own candidates' outcomes, drawing on anonymous accounts from staffers across multiple campaigns. S. Res. 708 covers Senate officers and employees; campaign staff, who work for the campaigns rather than the Senate itself, fall outside that definition. The Gillibrand-McCormick bill extends the prohibition to executive branch officials but similarly doesn't reach political operatives. ([NPR, May 7](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/07/nx-s1-5795891/prediction-markets-kalshi-polymarket-campaigns))

<ImplicationBox items={["Campaign staffers trade with real informational advantages — internal polling, fundraising velocity, scheduling data the market doesn't have. The legal framework for treating that as 'material nonpublic information' under the CEA is untested.", "S. Res. 708 and the Gillibrand-McCormick bill both leave campaign staff outside any trading prohibition. Closing that gap requires either a new bill or a CFTC rule defining which categories of political operatives are subject to insider-trading standards.", "Congressional pressure for a broader ban — covering campaign staff, consultants, and political operatives — is the logical next step; this reporting gives legislators a concrete example without requiring them to identify specific individuals."]} />

## Industry Outlook

Capital and courts moved in opposite directions this week. The $1 billion Series F represents institutional investors pricing state enforcement risk as bounded; the split between Arizona and Washington, two district judges reaching opposite conclusions on the same statute the same day, shows that district-level jurisprudence is nowhere near settled. The Ninth Circuit's Nevada ruling, when it comes, will be the first circuit-level answer on sports contract preemption outside the Third Circuit. Add the Senate Commerce hearing on May 20 and Minnesota's prediction-market ban heading to Governor Walz's desk, and the next two weeks carry more binary outcomes than any stretch since the ANPRM opened in March.
