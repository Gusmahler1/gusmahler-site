## Federal Courts

### Ho-Chunk Nation's IGRA claims against Kalshi survive federal court challenge

Judge William M. Conley of the Western District of Wisconsin on May 11 rejected Kalshi's argument that the Ho-Chunk Nation lacks a statutory right to sue third-party operators under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. The court relied on prior rulings holding that online gaming occurs where the bettor is physically located, not where a platform's servers are — meaning Ho-Chunk members using Kalshi from Wisconsin reservations are legally gambling on tribal lands, regardless of where the platform operates. Conley denied a preliminary injunction, finding no immediate irreparable harm, but the IGRA claims proceed to trial. A trial date has been set for May 24, 2027. ([Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/159692/wisconsin-judge-tribal-igra-claims-kalshi) · [WEAU, May 12](https://www.weau.com/2026/05/12/ho-chunk-nation-wins-federal-decision-kalshi-case/) · [bettorsinsider, May 12](https://bettorsinsider.com/sports-betting/2026/05/12/ho-chunk-nations-igra-case-against-kalshi-survives-federal-court-the-wisconsin-ruling-that-could-reshape-prediction-markets/))

<ImplicationBox items={["IGRA creates a separate legal theory from state preemption — one the CFTC's CEA exclusivity argument isn't designed to neutralize. Federal tribal gaming law predates the CEA and operates in parallel; a court holding that IGRA governs online betting on tribal lands can coexist with a ruling that state gambling law is preempted.", "The 'where the bettor is located' theory, if it holds through appeal, makes tribal land a jurisdictional trigger for any platform with users on reservations — regardless of where the platform is registered, incorporated, or hosted.", "A May 2027 trial date means this case runs past any ANPRM final rule. The CFTC's rulemaking won't resolve the IGRA question; it will have to be litigated on a separate track."]} />

### Four New Mexico pueblos file IGRA suit against Kalshi

The Mescalero Apache Tribe, Pueblo of Isleta, Pueblo of Pojoaque, and Pueblo of Sandia filed a 34-page complaint in U.S. District Court in Albuquerque on May 13 alleging that Kalshi is conducting illegal sports betting on tribal lands in violation of IGRA. The suit is the third tribal IGRA action against prediction-market platforms, after California and Wisconsin, and adds a fact-specific claim not present in the prior two: Kalshi allows users as young as 18 to trade sports contracts, while New Mexico law requires sports bettors to be at least 21 at tribal gaming facilities. ([Source New Mexico, May 13](https://sourcenm.com/2026/05/13/new-mexico-pueblos-sue-kalshi-alleging-prediction-market-violates-tribal-gaming-sovereignty/) · [Casinobeats, May 13](https://casinobeats.com/2026/05/13/new-mexico-tribes-sue-kalshi-minnesota-passes-ban-prediction-markets/) · [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/new-mexico-tribes-claim-kalshi-sports-markets-violate-federal-law/))

<ImplicationBox items={["The age-discrepancy argument doesn't turn on preemption theory at all. It's a concrete statutory claim: Kalshi is allowing users who couldn't legally bet at a tribal facility to do the functional equivalent from tribal land. That's a narrower, harder-to-dismiss theory.", "Three tribal IGRA suits across three states in a short window suggests a coordinated litigation strategy. The Ho-Chunk ruling surviving the motion-to-dismiss stage gives other tribes a procedural template and a favorable legal precedent to cite."]} />

## State Legislation

### Minnesota Legislature passes first statewide prediction-market ban

The Minnesota Legislature passed SF 4760 on May 12 — 57-9 in the Senate, 100-32 in the House — making it the first state to clear a full prediction-market ban through both chambers. The bill, framed as a public-safety measure, would make hosting or advertising a prediction market in Minnesota a felony. It's on Governor Tim Walz's desk; if signed or allowed to become law without action, it takes effect August 1. CFTC Chair Brian Selig responded directly: "If you interfere with the operation of federal law in regulating financial markets, we will sue you." ([MPR News, May 12](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/12/prediction-market-ban-on-sites-like-polymarket-kalshi-among-bills-edging-ahead-at-capitol) · [Covers, May 13](https://www.covers.com/industry/minnesota-prediction-market-ban-passed-legislature-may-13-2026) · [ingame](https://www.ingame.com/minnesota-legislature-passes-prediction-ban/) · [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/minnesota-banning-kalshi-polymarket-prediction-markets-what-to-know/601597303))

<ImplicationBox items={["Minnesota's ban matters less for what it can enforce inside the state than for what it proves is politically achievable. Other legislatures watching the CFTC preemption fight now have a concrete template: pass a ban, wait for the CFTC to sue, and let the courts decide.", "The 100-32 House margin is the telling figure. A blowout in both chambers signals genuine legislative consensus — this wasn't a close political call — and makes it harder for opponents to frame state bans as fringe positions.", "If Walz signs and the CFTC sues Minnesota, the agency takes on a fifth active state lawsuit at a moment when its workforce is already down roughly 24%. ## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC files Sixth Circuit amicus in KalshiEx v. Schuler

The CFTC filed an amicus brief on May 12 in KalshiEx v. Schuler, the Sixth Circuit appeal of a federal district court ruling in Ohio, asserting exclusive federal jurisdiction over prediction markets and arguing the district court took "an improperly narrow view" of the CEA's preemption scope. The CFTC is CFTC PR 9230-26. ([CFTC PR 9230-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9230-26) · [The Block, May 13](https://www.theblock.co/post/401039/cftc-backs-kalshi-in-ohio-fight-over-prediction-markets-jurisdictional-battle))

The filing is the agency's fifth active amicus or direct preemption action, alongside ongoing suits against Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, and New York, and the April 24 opposing brief at the Massachusetts SJC.

<ImplicationBox items={["The CFTC is now on the record in the Sixth Circuit with the same preemption argument it has pressed in every other venue. Circuit-court losses, when they come, will apply to the agency directly — not just to the private party on whose side it filed.", "The Schuler case runs through Ohio and the Sixth Circuit, geographically separate from the Third and Ninth circuit fights. A favorable ruling creates a third circuit endorsing CEA preemption; an adverse ruling sets up the cross-circuit split that would most directly invite Supreme Court review.", "The CFTC filing an amicus in a case where it isn't the plaintiff is a public commitment. It makes it harder for a future chair to walk back the preemption theory without explicitly reversing agency positions on the record in multiple circuits."]} />

### CFTC issues no-action letter on event contract reporting

The CFTC's Division of Market Oversight and Division of Clearing and Risk issued a no-action letter on May 13 stating the agency will not recommend enforcement against designated contract markets or derivatives clearing organizations for failure to comply with certain swap-related recordkeeping and swap data repository reporting requirements for fully collateralized event contracts. The practical effect: platforms and clearing organizations can operate their event contract businesses without accumulating swap reporting violations while the ANPRM process continues toward an eventual NPRM. ([Crypto Times, May 14](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/14/cftc-takes-no-action-stance-as-prediction-market-regulation-battle-intensifies/) · [The Block, May 14](https://www.theblock.co/post/401215/cftc-no-action-prediction-market) · [BanklessTimes](https://www.banklesstimes.com/articles/2026/05/14/cftc-issues-no-action-relief-for-event-contracts/))

<ImplicationBox items={["No-action letters are reversible and non-binding on courts, but they matter operationally. They allow platforms and clearing organizations to run their businesses without swap reporting violations accumulating while the formal rulemaking grinds forward.", "The letter clarifies the CFTC's posture in the regulatory gap between a closed comment period and a not-yet-drafted NPRM: provide operational cover administratively rather than let the legacy swap framework create compliance friction for a product category the rules weren't written to govern.", "No-action relief can be rescinded without notice by a future chair. ## Congressional & Legislative Activity

### House Oversight opens insider-trading investigation into prediction markets

Rep. Chris Pappas (D-NH) led seven House Democrats — Reps. Goodlander, Jacobs, Magaziner, Moulton, Titus, and Tlaib — in a May 11 letter to House Oversight Chair James Comer asking him to subpoena prediction-market platforms and investigate whether traders used classified information to profit ahead of U.S. military operations. Comer confirmed publicly that an investigation is underway and that subpoenas may follow. The specific trigger was reporting on a trader with a 93% success rate who netted roughly $1 million across three events involving U.S./Israeli operations against Iran (October 2024, June 2025, February 2026); a separate ring of 38 accounts netted more than $2 million on the February 28, 2026 strikes alone. ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/house/5873055-lawmakers-probe-insider-trading-markets/) · [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/160204/comer-house-oversight-prediction-market-probe-subpoenas) · [Pappas press release](https://pappas.house.gov/media/press-releases/after-pappas-calls-for-us-house-to-subpoena-prediction-markets-comer-confirms-investigation-in-progress-subpoenas-may-follow))

<ImplicationBox items={["A committee chair confirming an active investigation is a different order of pressure than a letter or a floor speech. ### Sixty-three House members back trading ban; Schiff accuses CFTC chair of "duplicity"

Rep. Alexander Vindman led a letter from 62 colleagues on May 14 calling on Congress to ban members from trading on prediction markets — the largest House coalition on the issue, adding to the Hinson and Krishnamoorthi-Cloud resolutions from last week. No floor vote is scheduled on any of the three measures. Separately, Sen. Adam Schiff appeared at a Brookings Institution event on May 12-13 and accused CFTC Chair Brian Selig of "duplicity" over the agency's handling of prediction markets, arguing sports contracts are gambling and CFTC oversight is insufficient. Selig, responding to the characterization, said prediction markets and sports betting are "two separate things." ## Market Integrity

### Platforms reported to have flagged more than 400 suspicious trades in 2026

Reuters reported on May 15, citing people familiar with the matter, that prediction-market platforms have collectively flagged more than 400 suspicious trades so far in 2026 — more than twice the count for all of 2025. Some of those referrals have gone to the CFTC. Polymarket reported a similar surge in flagged activity. ## International

### Kalshi and Polymarket continue operating in India despite PROGA ban

Bloomberg reported on May 18 that both platforms are still accepting Indian users despite India's PROGA ban, which took effect May 1. A single Indian Premier League match on May 7 drew more than $27 million in combined trading across the two platforms from Indian users. India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology has not yet moved against payment processors or the platforms' India-facing interfaces. ([Bloomberg, May 18](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-18/kalshi-polymarket-defy-india-ban-on-online-betting-platforms))

## Industry Outlook

Three pressure fronts converged this week. The Ho-Chunk ruling and the New Mexico tribes' suit prove that tribal sovereignty is a parallel legal vector to state preemption — one the CFTC's amicus campaign isn't built to neutralize. Minnesota became the first state to clear a full prediction-market ban through both chambers, which matters less for what it can enforce inside Minnesota than for what it establishes as a template other state legislatures will copy. The CFTC's no-action letter signals the agency's posture in the gap between a closed comment period and a not-yet-drafted NPRM: operational relief now, formal rulemaking later. The Senate Commerce hearing on May 20 and the still-pending Ninth Circuit Nevada decision are the next two binary events.
