## Market Integrity

### Polymarket insider trading scrutiny grows, with documented cases tied to geopolitical events

Multiple outlets this week documented what appear to be well-timed bets on Polymarket that preceded geopolitical events by minutes or hours. NPR reported on March 1 that a single account made $553,000 betting on the death of Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei shortly before an Israeli strike killed him. A Snopes investigation published March 3 analyzed a broader pattern: the same account and related accounts had won 93% of their five-figure wagers on events related to Iran — including the timing of U.S. and Israeli military operations — over a sustained period. Polymarket's rules at the time did not explicitly prohibit trading while in possession of material non-public information, a gap that the CFTC's February 25 enforcement advisory had put platforms on notice about. ([NPR, March 1](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/01/nx-s1-5731568/polymarket-trade-iran-supreme-leader-killing)  ·  [Snopes, March 3](https://www.snopes.com/news/2026/03/03/polymarket-bets-iran-strikes/))

<ImplicationBox items={["The Polymarket cases are precisely the scenario the CFTC's February 25 enforcement advisory was designed to address — and they are occurring on a platform that is not a CFTC-registered DCM. That distinction matters: DCMs are subject to the CEA's Core Principles requiring surveillance and anti-manipulation programs, while offshore or non-registered platforms operate under different legal requirements. The political fallout, however, is not well-calibrated to legal distinctions, and Congressional scrutiny of the entire industry tends to follow high-profile scandals regardless of which platform they occur on."]} />

## State-Level Legislative Activity

### Eleven states have introduced prediction market legislation; states cite $600 million in lost tax revenue

A Stateline analysis published March 6 documented that at least eleven states had introduced legislation targeting prediction markets in 2026, with approaches ranging from full categorical bans to licensing and taxation frameworks. The piece framed the state interest partly in fiscal terms: states estimate the prediction market industry is generating significant revenue that flows to federally regulated platforms without triggering state gaming taxes, at a cost of roughly $600 million in annual tax revenue that would otherwise accrue to state coffers. Hawaii and Kentucky were identified as having advanced bills furthest through their respective legislative processes. Iowa and New York were considering regulatory and taxation frameworks, while Hawaii and Illinois were pursuing outright restrictions. ([Stateline, March 6](https://stateline.org/2026/03/06/kalshi-and-polymarket-are-skirting-laws-on-sports-betting-states-say/))

<ImplicationBox items={["The revenue angle reframes the state challenge from a values-based dispute (gambling as vice) to a fiscal one — and fiscal arguments tend to generate broader legislative coalitions. States that might otherwise be indifferent to prediction markets on policy grounds have a concrete financial incentive to impose licensing requirements. A state licensing regime, even if nominally permissive, would add significant compliance overhead and fragment the national market that platforms relying on federal preemption are currently building."]} />

### State legislative landscape: Utah and Iowa as lead cases

Utah's HB243, which passed both chambers last week, was awaiting formal legislative enrollment and transmission to Governor Cox — who has publicly committed to signing it. The bill takes an exclusionary approach: it redefines proposition betting as gambling rather than creating a licensing pathway. Iowa's SF 2085, introduced by Sen. Mike Klimesh and referred to the Senate Ways and Means Committee, takes the opposite tack: it would permit prediction markets to operate in Iowa, but only under a state license. The licensing fee is $10 million upfront, with annual renewals at $100,000 and a 20% tax on gross revenue from Iowa residents. Both bills illustrate the range of state approaches, from prohibition to high-cost regulation, and both are designed to neutralize the federal preemption argument by establishing state authority independently of CFTC registration. ([Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/prediction-markets-in-iowa-could-soon-need-state-license/)  ·  [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/gaming/gaming-regulation/us-state-prediction-markets-legislation-analysis/))

## DCM Application Pipeline

### Sporttrade files for DCM and DCO registration with CFTC

Sporttrade, which holds sports betting licenses in Arizona, Colorado, Iowa, New Jersey, and Virginia, filed applications with the CFTC for both designated contract market (DCM) and derivatives clearing organization (DCO) registration in early February. If approved, Sporttrade would be positioned to offer event contracts nationally under the federal preemption framework. The dual application — for both the exchange and clearing functions — reflects a more vertically integrated approach than other applicants in the current queue. Novig (via Ludlow Exchange) and Smarkets are among the other platforms with pending DCM applications. The CFTC has noted that DCM applications have more than doubled over the past year, with the majority from entities primarily seeking to operate prediction markets. ([SBC Americas, February 4](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/02/04/sporttrade-applies-cftc-registration/)  ·  [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/sporttrade-files-for-dcm-status-under-cftc-to-put-the-consumer-first/))

<ImplicationBox items={["The DCM application wave is proceeding in parallel with the legal battle over whether DCM status actually confers the preemption protection platforms are counting on. If courts ultimately rule against preemption, the value of a DCM license shrinks considerably — from a national operating license to a federal registration that still requires state-by-state compliance. Platforms building DCM applications are making an implicit bet that the preemption argument holds."]} />
