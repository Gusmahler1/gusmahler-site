## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC Launches Innovation Task Force with Prediction Markets as Explicit Focus

CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig announced the formation of an Innovation Task Force on March 24, designating prediction markets and event contracts as one of three core focus areas alongside crypto assets and artificial intelligence. The task force will develop regulatory frameworks for each area and coordinate with other federal agencies, including the Securities and Exchange Commission. It will be led by Michael J. Passalacqua, a senior adviser to the chairman, and will work in partnership with the Commission's Innovation Advisory Committee.

Selig framed the task force as a framework-setting mechanism: "By establishing a clear regulatory framework for innovators building on the new frontier of finance, we can foster responsible innovation at home and ensure American market participants are not left on the sidelines." The announcement came twelve days after the CFTC opened an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) on event contracts, with the public comment period running through April 30.

([CFTC Press Release 9201-26, March 24](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9201-26)  ·  [Decrypt, March 24](https://decrypt.co/362212/cftc-unveils-innovation-task-force-crypto-ai-prediction-markets))

<ImplicationBox items={["The task force signals that the CFTC intends to lead the regulatory framing of prediction markets rather than cede that ground to Congress or state enforcement bodies. A dedicated regulatory framework could clarify which contract types are permissible and under what conditions — providing the kind of legal certainty the industry has sought. The timing, launched the same day as the Schiff-Curtis bill introduction, reflects competing federal visions: one branch working to define a viable regulatory structure while another moves to restrict or ban the industry outright."]} />

## Congressional & Legislative Activity

### Three Restrictive Bills Introduced in One Week as Congressional Pressure Escalates

The week of March 23 saw three separate prediction markets bills introduced in Congress — spanning both chambers and both parties — each targeting a different dimension of the industry's operations. None has yet received a committee hearing or vote.

#### Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act (Schiff-Curtis)

Senators Adam Schiff (D-CA) and John Curtis (R-UT) introduced the bipartisan Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act, which would amend the Commodity Exchange Act to bar any CFTC-registered entity from listing contracts that closely resemble sports bets or casino-style games. The bill targets the regulatory structure that has allowed prediction markets to offer sports-related contracts under CFTC oversight while bypassing state gaming laws and tribal gaming compacts. Co-sponsors include Senators Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), Richard Blumenthal (D-CT), Chris Van Hollen (D-MD), and Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI) — the same four who co-sponsored the STOP Corrupt Bets Act introduced three days later.

Sponsors characterize the bill as closing a regulatory "backdoor" that enables platforms to sidestep state consumer protections and gaming taxes. Curtis and Schiff appeared jointly on CNBC to make the case for the legislation.

([Senator Curtis press release, March 23](https://www.curtis.senate.gov/press-releases/curtis-schiff-introduce-bipartisan-legislation-to-ban-sports-prediction-market-contracts/)  ·  [Senator Schiff press release, March 23](https://www.schiff.senate.gov/news/press-releases/news-sens-schiff-curtis-introduce-bipartisan-legislation-to-ban-sports-prediction-market-contracts/)  ·  [CNBC, March 25](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/25/prediction-markets-bill-insider-trading-kalshi-polymarket-adam-schiff-john-curtis.html))

#### PREDICT Act (Smith-Budzinski)

Representatives Adrian Smith (R-NE) and Nikki Budzinski (D-IL) introduced the Preventing Real-time Exploitation and Deceptive Insider Congressional Trading Act (PREDICT Act), a bipartisan House bill targeting a narrower problem: government insiders trading on prediction markets using nonpublic information. The bill would prohibit members of Congress, their spouses and dependent children, the President, Vice President, and political appointees from trading on political events, policy decisions, and government actions. Violations would result in a civil penalty of 10 percent of the transaction value plus full disgorgement of profits to the U.S. Treasury.

([Rep. Smith press release, March 25](https://adriansmith.house.gov/media/press-releases/smith-budzinski-introduce-bipartisan-bill-ban-members-congress-federal)  ·  [Rep. Budzinski press release, March 25](https://budzinski.house.gov/posts/budzinski-and-smith-introduce-bill-to-ban-members-of-congress-federal-officials-from-insider-prediction-market-trading))

#### STOP Corrupt Bets Act (Merkley-Raskin)

Senator Jeff Merkley (D-OR) and Representative Jamie Raskin (D-MD), joined by Senators Warren, Blumenthal, Van Hollen, and Whitehouse, introduced the STOP Corrupt Bets Act — the most sweeping bill of the three. The bicameral legislation would prohibit event contracts tied to elections, war, and government actions, direct the CFTC to block markets without genuine hedging purposes, and require a Government Accountability Office study on prediction markets and insider trading. Merkley stated that prediction markets covering government decisions are "ripe for corruption and erodes public trust" when any participant can trade on nonpublic information about government actions.

([Rep. Raskin press release, March 26](https://raskin.house.gov/press-releases?id=F933A3B6-436B-45F4-B9A0-C58D2615E158)  ·  [Senator Merkley press release, March 26](https://www.merkley.senate.gov/new-merkley-raskin-legislation-bans-gambling-on-elections-sports-war-and-government-activity/)  ·  [Axios, March 26](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/26/prediction-market-ban-bill-jeff-merkley))

<ImplicationBox items={["Three bills in five days — bipartisan, bicameral, targeting sports contracts, political contracts, and government official participation — signals that Congress is no longer treating prediction markets as a fringe regulatory question. The three approaches are not mutually exclusive: all three could be enacted independently, compounding restrictions on the industry. The PREDICT Act's narrow scope on government officials has the most cross-aisle support; the STOP Corrupt Bets Act's breadth would, if enacted, effectively eliminate the two contract types driving the largest share of platform volume. None of the bills has yet advanced to committee markup."]} />

## State-Level Activity

### California Governor Newsom Issues Executive Order on Prediction Market Insider Trading

Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order on March 27 prohibiting all gubernatorial appointees from using confidential or nonpublic information obtained through their official roles to profit — or to assist others in profiting — from prediction markets. The ban extends to transactions made on behalf of spouses, children, other family members, and business partners. It takes effect immediately and applies to all individuals in state positions appointed by the governor's office.

The governor's office cited documented incidents of apparent insider trading on offshore prediction platforms as the impetus: including a trader who made $410,000 betting on the U.S. military capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro hours before the operation became public, and six individuals who collectively netted $1.2 million on contracts predicting a U.S. strike against Iran. The state legislature had not yet acted on prediction market legislation, making the executive order California's first formal restriction on participation in the industry by state officials.

([California Governor's Office, March 27](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/03/27/governor-newsom-strengthens-bans-on-insider-betting-by-state-officials-takes-aim-at-corruption-fueled-by-trump/)  ·  [Decrypt, March 27](https://decrypt.co/362598/gavin-newsom-bans-california-officials-prediction-market-insider-trading))

<ImplicationBox items={["Newsom's action does not regulate the platforms themselves — it restricts the conduct of California state officials. But it establishes a state-level policy precedent that other governors can replicate without legislative action, potentially creating a patchwork of varying participation restrictions across states even where direct enforcement against platforms remains legally contested. The cited insider trading incidents — involving military and foreign policy actions — illustrate the specific nonpublic information categories that have drawn the most scrutiny and that the STOP Corrupt Bets Act and PREDICT Act both target directly."]} />

## Market Integrity & Compliance

### Kalshi and Polymarket Announce Insider Trading Guardrails

Both platforms announced new technological controls on March 23, the same day as the Schiff-Curtis bill introduction. Kalshi stated it would preemptively block political candidates from trading on their own campaigns and bar individuals involved in college or professional sports — players, coaches, team employees — from trading on markets related to their sport. Polymarket announced parallel measures improving its market integrity rules. Both platforms also said they were expanding surveillance tooling to monitor for unusual trading patterns associated with insider information.

The announcements followed months of congressional attention to documented incidents on offshore exchanges, including large pre-event profits on contracts tied to Venezuelan and Iranian military actions. Neither platform specified the technical mechanisms for verifying prohibited individuals' identities, or how the controls would apply to indirect trading through family members or corporate entities.

([U.S. News, March 23](https://www.usnews.com/news/business/articles/2026-03-23/kalshi-and-polymarket-rush-to-ban-insider-trading-as-senators-move-to-curb-prediction-markets)  ·  [Yahoo Finance, March 23](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/kalshi-polymarket-rush-ban-211555260.html))

<ImplicationBox items={["Coordinated same-day announcements on the day of a major legislative introduction reflect a calculated industry response: preempt the argument that platforms are not self-policing. The legislative record will include these announcements, and sponsors of the Schiff-Curtis bill explicitly noted the same day that the new restrictions were insufficient. The gap between platform-level controls and the scope of legislative proposals — which would ban entire contract categories regardless of who is trading — suggests that compliance gestures alone are unlikely to redirect the congressional debate."]} />

## Company News

### 5(c) Capital Launches $35M Prediction Markets Venture Fund, Backed by Industry CEOs

A new sector-focused venture capital fund, 5(c) Capital, announced a $35 million fundraise targeting early-stage companies in the prediction markets ecosystem. The fund is led by Adhi Rajaprabhakaran, a former Kalshi trader, and Noah Zingler-Sternig, Kalshi's former head of operations. Despite their public rivalry, the CEOs of both major prediction market platforms — Tarek Mansour of Kalshi and Shayne Coplan of Polymarket — are backing the fund, as are Marc Andreessen and Ribbit Capital. The fund's name references Section 5(c) of the Commodity Exchange Act, the statutory provision governing designated contract markets under which CFTC-regulated prediction market platforms operate.

The announcement came the same week as three restrictive congressional bills — a juxtaposition that reflects the split character of the current moment: sustained venture capital and industry-level investment commitment alongside intensifying legislative and regulatory pressure. Kalshi had closed a $1 billion raise at a $22 billion valuation the prior week.

([TechCrunch, March 23](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/23/despite-bitter-rivalry-kalshi-polymarket-ceos-back-35m-predictions-markets-vc-fund/)  ·  [Bloomberg, March 23](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-23/kalshi-polymarket-founders-back-new-prediction-market-vc-fund)  ·  [Fortune, March 23](https://fortune.com/2026/03/23/kalshi-polymarket-5cc-capital-prediction-market-fund-raise/))

<ImplicationBox items={["A dedicated sector VC fund signals investor conviction that prediction markets represent a durable category worth building around, not a regulatory flash-in-the-pan.
