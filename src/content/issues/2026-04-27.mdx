## Market Integrity

### CFTC and DOJ file first insider trading charges for event contracts

The CFTC filed a civil complaint in SDNY and the DOJ announced parallel criminal charges against Gannon Ken Van Dyke, a soldier who used classified knowledge of a covert Venezuela operation to purchase 436,000 "Yes" shares on Polymarket's "Maduro Out by January 31, 2026?" contract in late December 2025. Trading under the handle "Burdensome-Mix," he made roughly $404,000 when the contract resolved. The CFTC's complaint, filed April 23, seeks disgorgement, civil penalties, and a permanent trading ban. ([CFTC PR 9217-26, April 23](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9217-26) · [CNBC, April 23](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/23/doj-soldier-polymarket-bets-venezuela-maduro.html))

Polymarket confirmed it cooperated with investigators. Kalshi stated it had independently identified and blocked Van Dyke before the charges were announced. Van Dyke was released pending proceedings. ([CNBC, April 24](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/24/special-forces-van-dyke-polymarket-bets-maduro-raid-kalshi.html))

### Kalshi suspends candidates; both platforms codify insider trading bans

Kalshi fined and suspended three federal candidates who had traded on markets tied to their own races. The platform did not name them. Both Kalshi and Polymarket then announced expanded policies: politicians barred from trading on their own races, athletes from their own leagues, employees from contracts tied to their employers. ([Fortune, April 26](https://fortune.com/2026/04/26/prediction-markets-insider-trading-illegal-kalshi-polymarket-robin-hanson-economist/) · [Bloomberg, April 25](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-25/insider-trading-cases-threaten-reckoning-for-prediction-markets))

<ImplicationBox items={["Self-trading by candidates was real and detectable — moving policy from terms-of-service deterrence to enforceable rules removes the most politically vulnerable attack surface.", "Platform-level enforcement ahead of CFTC rulemaking is the industry's best argument against prescriptive federal rules on integrity."]} />

## Company News

### Polymarket and Kalshi launch perpetual futures, targeting offshore exchanges

Polymarket opened a waitlist for perpetual futures on April 21 — crypto and stocks, 10x leverage, no expiration. Kalshi launched its version at a New York event under the internal codename "Timeless," starting with Bitcoin and adding stablecoin collateral in Q2. Both platforms' CFTC-registered DCM status gives them a domestic distribution advantage over offshore competitors. ([Bloomberg, April 21](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-21/kalshi-to-expand-crypto-wagers-with-perpetual-futures-push) · [CNBC, April 21](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/21/polymarket-launches-trading-of-heavily-leveraged-perps-contracts.html) · [Unchained, April 22](https://unchainedcrypto.com/kalshi-and-polymarket-race-to-launch-crypto-perpetual-futures-challenging-coinbase-and-robinhood/))

### Polymarket in talks to raise $400M at a $15B valuation

Polymarket is seeking $400M at a $15B valuation, up 66% from the $9B ICE-backed round last year, but roughly one-third below Kalshi's $22B. The discount likely reflects Kalshi's ~90% U.S. market share, longer fee history, and cleaner domestic regulatory profile. Polymarket crossed $10B in monthly trading volume in March 2026 for the first time. ([Bloomberg, April 20](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-20/polymarket-in-talks-for-new-investment-at-15-billion-valuation) · [Fortune, April 21](https://fortune.com/2026/04/21/polymarket-valuation-discount-kalshi-crypto/))

## International

### Brazil bans non-economic event contracts, blocks 27 platforms

Brazil's National Monetary Council issued Resolution No. 5,298 on April 24, banning derivative contracts tied to non-economic events — sports, elections, cultural outcomes. Telecom regulator Anatel was instructed to block 27 platforms including Kalshi and Polymarket. Finance Minister Dario Durigan cited investor protection; Economic Reforms Secretary Regis Dudena said the products were "financial instruments that resemble betting in practice." ([Bloomberg, April 24](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-24/brazil-moves-to-ban-prediction-markets-on-elections-sports) · [iGaming Today, April 24](https://www.igamingtoday.com/brazil-blocks-27-prediction-market-platforms-in-crackdown-on-unregulated-betting/))

<ImplicationBox items={["The \"financial instrument, not gambling\" argument is losing ground internationally — Brazil's framing matches what U.S. state gaming regulators are arguing in court.", "U.S. platforms are insulated by CFTC registration; unregistered platforms operating outside U.S. jurisdiction have no equivalent shield.", "Brazil provides a legislative template; other jurisdictions are likely watching."]} />

## Federal v. State Litigation

### CFTC sues New York to defend exclusive jurisdiction over event contracts

The CFTC filed a complaint in the Southern District of New York on April 24, seeking a declaratory judgment that the Commodity Exchange Act grants it exclusive authority over event contracts and a permanent injunction preventing New York from enforcing state gambling laws against CFTC-registered platforms. The action follows New York's cease-and-desist letters and civil enforcement moves earlier in the week targeting Coinbase- and Gemini-run platforms. The agency now has four active preemption lawsuits, against Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, and New York, in addition to its ongoing defense of Kalshi in state-court litigation. ([CFTC PR 9218-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9218-26) · [CoinDesk, April 24](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/04/24/u-s-cftc-adds-new-york-to-string-of-states-its-suing-to-stop-prediction-market-pushback))

<ImplicationBox items={["The CFTC's litigation strategy depends on quick wins to set precedent before its workforce shortfall limits capacity to fight on multiple fronts simultaneously.", "Each new state suit broadens the matrix that will eventually require Supreme Court resolution; the question is which case reaches certiorari first.", "New York's cease-and-desist approach against Coinbase- and Gemini-run platforms tests whether the CFTC's preemption argument extends to crypto-native exchanges operating outside DCM registration."]} />

### Wisconsin attorney general sues five platforms for unlicensed sports betting

Wisconsin Attorney General Josh Kaul filed suit against Kalshi, Coinbase, Polymarket, Robinhood, and Crypto.com on April 24, asking a state court to declare the companies in violation of Wisconsin gambling law, classify their operations as a public nuisance, and bar them from offering sports-related event contracts to Wisconsin customers. The complaint quotes the platforms' own marketing as evidence: Kalshi's Instagram ads describing the platform as "The First Nationwide Legal Sports Betting Platform" and Polymarket's self-description as "a platform where people can bet on the outcome of future events." Kalshi and the other named platforms maintain their contracts are swaps listed on a CFTC-registered exchange and fall under federal jurisdiction. ([CoinDesk, April 24](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/04/24/wisconsin-joins-prediction-market-fight-suing-kalshi-coinbase-polymarket-robinhood-and-crypto-com) · [WPR, April 25](https://www.wpr.org/sports/wisconsin-doj-lawsuit-kalshi-prediction-markets-illegal-online-sports-betting))

## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC workforce down 24% as prediction markets oversight demands grow

The CFTC has lost roughly 24% of its workforce since January 2025. The agency is simultaneously managing three active preemption lawsuits, closing the ANPRM comment period on April 30, and now running its first event contract insider trading enforcement action. ([CNN Politics, April 26](https://us.cnn.com/2026/04/26/politics/commodity-futures-trading-commission-shrinking-prediction-markets))

## Industry Outlook

Three dominant threads run through this week: the Van Dyke charges, the Brazil ban, and the same-day CFTC and Wisconsin filings. Each tests a different angle of the industry's "legitimate financial market" framing: law enforcement domestically, regulators internationally, and state attorneys general acting in coordination. The platforms' response (proactive enforcement, new policy rollouts, perpetual futures diversification) suggests they understand the risk, but whether those moves are sufficient to shape the ANPRM outcome will become clearer after April 30. The Ninth Circuit's pending sports preemption decision remains the highest-stakes single variable. A ruling against the platforms would likely accelerate a SCOTUS petition and leave the sports contract business in limbo for another two to three years.
