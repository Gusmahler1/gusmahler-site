## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC issues staff guidance and opens rulemaking on prediction markets

On March 12, the CFTC's Division of Market Oversight issued two simultaneous releases. The first was a staff advisory reminding designated contract markets (DCMs) of their existing obligations under the Commodity Exchange Act — specifically Core Principles 3, 4, and 12, which require platforms to list only contracts not readily susceptible to manipulation, conduct real-time surveillance for disorderly trading, and protect markets from abusive practices. The advisory also clarified that the CEA's anti-manipulation and insider trading prohibitions apply directly to prediction market contracts, and specifically instructed DCMs listing sports-related event contracts to coordinate with relevant sports governing bodies when developing compliance and oversight programs.

The second release was an Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) seeking public comment on whether the existing regulatory framework needs to be amended. Key questions include: what types of contracts should be prohibited on public-interest grounds, whether margin trading should be permitted on prediction market platforms, and whether individuals with insider knowledge of an event should be barred from trading related contracts. Comments are due April 30. ([CFTC Staff Advisory Press Release, March 12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9193-26)  ·  [CFTC ANPRM Press Release, March 12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9194-26)  ·  [Federal Register, March 16](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/16/2026-05105/prediction-markets))

## Congressional & Legislative Activity

### Two bills introduced targeting specific prediction market contract types

Congress introduced two contract-specific bills this week, both taking narrower approaches than wholesale industry regulation.

#### DEATH BETS Act — Sen. Schiff, March 10

Sen. Adam Schiff introduced legislation that would amend the Commodity Exchange Act to categorically ban CFTC-registered platforms from listing contracts referencing terrorism, assassination, war, or death. The key mechanism: it removes the CFTC's current case-by-case discretion to approve such contracts, making the prohibition statutory rather than regulatory. The bill was introduced two days before the CFTC's own ANPRM, suggesting Congressional intent to close off any permissive stance the agency might take on geopolitical contracts through rulemaking. ([CoinDesk, March 11](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/11/schiff-pushes-ban-on-prediction-market-bets-tied-to-war-and-death)  ·  [Sen. Schiff's office](https://www.schiff.senate.gov/news/press-releases/news-sens-schiff-curtis-introduce-bipartisan-legislation-to-ban-sports-prediction-market-contracts/))

#### Prediction Markets Security and Integrity Act — Sen. Blumenthal

Sen. Richard Blumenthal introduced legislation with three main provisions: enhanced insider trading restrictions on prediction market platforms, stronger customer protections, and — most consequentially — a clause that would explicitly reverse the CFTC's position that federal registration preempts state gambling laws. If enacted, that provision would mean a CFTC-registered platform could still be subject to enforcement under state gaming statutes, eliminating the legal protection federally registered platforms currently rely on to operate nationally without state licensing. ([Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/bets-off-act-latest-federal-bill-targeting-prediction-markets-trades-involving-war-and-terrorism)  ·  [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/cftc-and-division-of-market-oversight-issue-two-releases-on-prediction-markets-and-event-contracts))

## Industry Context

### CFTC DCM applications more than doubled over the past year

The CFTC noted in its March 12 releases that applications to register as designated contract markets have more than doubled over the past year, with the majority from entities seeking to operate primarily as prediction markets. Novig (filing as Ludlow Exchange) and Sporttrade are both among the applicants in the current queue. The surge in applications is the backdrop against which both the advisory and the ANPRM were issued — the CFTC is standardizing its expectations before a new wave of platforms comes online.
