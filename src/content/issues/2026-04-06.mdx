## Federal Courts

### Third Circuit holds CEA preempts state gambling law for CFTC-registered platforms

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit issued a 2-1 opinion on April 6 in *KalshiEX LLC v. Flaherty* (No. 25-1922), affirming a preliminary injunction that prevents New Jersey gaming officials from enforcing state gambling laws against Kalshi's sports event contracts. Judge David J. Porter, joined by Chief Judge Michael A. Chagares, held that Kalshi's sports event contracts qualify as "swaps" under the Commodity Exchange Act, and that the CEA both field-preempts state regulation (meaning federal law occupies the regulatory space entirely, leaving no room for state law to operate) and conflict-preempts it (meaning New Jersey's gambling law directly conflicts with the federal framework) — the first federal appellate court to reach that conclusion. ([Skadden, April 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/04/third-circuit-affirms-kalshis-preliminary-injunction)  ·  [Holland & Knight, April 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/federal-appeals-court-cftc-jurisdiction-over-sports-event-contracts))

Judge Jane Roth dissented, arguing that Kalshi's products are "virtually indistinguishable from the betting products available on online sportsbooks" and that the presumption against preemption should apply with special force in areas of traditional state authority. The ruling affirms a preliminary injunction — the court found Kalshi "likely to succeed on the merits," not a final merits holding — and the case continues in the district court.

### Federal court halts Arizona's criminal prosecution under Supremacy Clause

Following last week's CFTC preemption suit against Arizona, U.S. District Judge Michael Liburdi granted a temporary restraining order on April 10 barring Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes from proceeding with the state's 20-count criminal misdemeanor case against Kalshi. The TRO runs through April 24. Judge Liburdi wrote that "The CFTC has demonstrated a reasonable chance of success in showing that the [Commodity Exchange] Act, at a minimum, field preempts Arizona law. Defendants' enforcement of Arizona's gambling laws therefore violates the Supremacy Clause of the United States Constitution." ([CoinDesk, April 10](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/04/10/federal-judge-blocks-arizona-from-bringing-criminal-charges-against-kalshi)  ·  [Arizona Mirror, April 10](https://azmirror.com/briefs/federal-judge-blocks-arizona-ag-from-prosecuting-kalshi-says-it-violates-the-constitution/))

## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC Innovation Task Force staffed, begins prediction markets rulemaking work

The CFTC announced the five members of its Innovation Task Force on April 10 (PR 9210-26). The task force, established by Chairman Selig to develop regulatory frameworks for crypto assets, AI, and prediction markets, is led by senior adviser Michael J. Passalacqua. Staff members are Hank Balaban, Sam Canavos, Mark Fajfar, Eugene Gonzalez IV, and Dina Moussa. The ANPRM public comment period closes April 30, giving the task force immediate pressure to begin synthesizing stakeholder input. ([CFTC PR 9210-26, April 10](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9210-26)  ·  [Venable, April 2026](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/04/cftc-launches-innovation-task-force-with))

## State-Level Activity

### Minnesota Senate committee advances felony ban on prediction market operators

The Minnesota Senate Commerce and Consumer Protection Committee advanced SF 4511 on April 10, moving the bill to the Senate Finance Committee for further review. Sponsored by Sen. John Marty, the bill would make operating a prediction market in Minnesota a felony punishable by up to five years in prison and a $10,000 fine. It removes prediction markets from an existing statutory exemption for futures exchanges, expressly classifying event contracts as illegal gambling. The bill extends criminal liability beyond platform operators to payment processors, data providers, oddsmakers, and media affiliates marketing the platforms within the state. If passed and signed before the legislature's May 18 adjournment, the ban would take effect August 1, 2026. ([SF 4511, Minnesota Legislature](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/bill.php?b=Senate&f=SF4511&ssn=0&y=2026)  ·  [KTTC, April 10](https://www.kttc.com/2026/04/10/bill-make-prediction-markets-illegal-mn-clears-first-senate-committee/)  ·  [DeFi Rate, April 2026](https://defirate.com/news/minnesota-senate-advances-prediction-market-ban-challenges-derivatives-classification/))
