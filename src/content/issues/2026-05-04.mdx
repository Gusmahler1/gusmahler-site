## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC ANPRM comment period closes with record participation

The CFTC's advance notice of proposed rulemaking on event contracts and prediction markets (RIN 3038-AF65) closed for public comment on April 30 with more than 1,500 submissions on the docket. The mix is the story. Industry filings sat next to state gaming regulators, tribal gaming operators, sports leagues, traditional sportsbooks, and academics, with positions ranging from full federal preemption of state gambling law to a categorical ban on sports event contracts. Kalshi ran an open comment campaign at comments-campaign.kalshi.com that let users draft and submit letters in support of the platform, contributing a meaningful share of the industry-side volume. ([CFTC PR 9194-26, March 12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9194-26) · [Federal Register, March 16](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/16/2026-05105/prediction-markets) · [Akin Gump, May 14](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cftc-receives-extensive-feedback-on-prediction-markets-rulemaking) · [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/ad8a494a/prediction-markets-at-a-crossroads-preemption-enforcement-and-rulemaking))

The next move sits with the Commission. Chair Selig has not committed to a timeline for a notice of proposed rulemaking, and the agency's workforce is down roughly 24% from January 2025, which limits how many fronts it can advance at once.

## Congressional & Legislative Activity

### Senate unanimously bans members and staff from trading on prediction markets

The Senate adopted S. Res. 708 by unanimous consent on April 30, prohibiting senators, officers, and employees from trading on prediction markets effective immediately. The resolution was sponsored by Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH), who told colleagues on the floor that "United States Senators have no business engaging in speculative activities like prediction markets while collecting a taxpayer-funded paycheck. Serving in Congress is an honor, not a side hustle." ([S. Res. 708](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/708) · [Roll Call, April 30](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/senate-bans-prediction-market-trading-members-staff/) · [CNBC, April 30](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/30/senate-prediction-markets-trading-ban-kalshi-polymarket.html))

Both Kalshi and Polymarket publicly supported the measure. Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour called it "a great step to increase trust in our markets by making it an industry standard." Polymarket said in a statement that "we're in full support of this." ([CNBC, April 30](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/30/senate-prediction-markets-trading-ban-kalshi-polymarket.html) · [Mediaite, April 30](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/senate-passes-resolution-banning-senators-and-staff-from-trading-on-prediction-markets-effective-immediately/))

On May 3, Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer called on the House and the White House to enact parallel bans. He cited reporting that 16 accounts each earned more than $100,000 by correctly predicting a U.S. military strike on Iran in the hours before it became public. House Majority Leader Steve Scalise told Semafor the House "will surely take a look at it"; House Administration Chair Bryan Steil (R-WI) said his committee had been "working on" rule language. The White House did not engage Schumer's specific request. ([Bloomberg, May 3](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-03/schumer-says-house-white-house-must-ban-prediction-market-bets) · [The Hill, May 3](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/5862159-chuck-schumer-bets-national-security-risk/) · [Schumer release, May 3](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/about-senate-dems/dpcc/press-releases/leader-schumer-calls-on-trump-and-house-to-ban-government-officials-from-wagering-on-prediction-markets-no-wagering-on-predicting-war-budgets-politics-and-legislation) · [Semafor, May 1](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/01/2026/house-may-ban-lawmakers-from-prediction-markets))

### Gillibrand and McCormick introduce the Prediction Market Act of 2026

Sens. Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY) and Dave McCormick (R-PA) introduced the Prediction Market Act of 2026 on May 1. The bill extends an insider-trading prohibition beyond the Senate to House members, the President, the Vice President, and senior executive branch officials. It directs the CFTC to define enforceable insider-trading standards specific to prediction markets, including a ban on trading on material nonpublic information. And it authorizes the CFTC to bar contracts on assassination, terrorism, war, violence, and other categories the agency deems contrary to the public interest on a case-by-case basis. ([Gillibrand release, May 1](https://www.gillibrand.senate.gov/news/press/release/senators-gillibrand-and-mccormick-introduce-bipartisan-bill-to-stop-politicians-from-profiting-off-insider-information-and-protect-consumers-in-prediction-markets/) · [McCormick release, May 1](https://www.mccormick.senate.gov/news/press-releases/senators-mccormick-gillibrand-introduce-legislation-to-strengthen-prediction-markets-and-protect-everyday-investors/) · [Bill text, May 1](https://www.mccormick.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/The-Prediction-Market-Act-of-2026.pdf))

McCormick framed the bill as a "transparent regulatory framework" with "strong safeguards for retail investors," a posture closer to industry alignment than to the Schiff-Curtis "Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act" moving in parallel through the Senate.

## Federal v. State Litigation

### 38 state attorneys general and the CFTC file opposing amicus briefs at Mass SJC

A coalition of 38 state attorneys general filed an amicus brief at the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court on April 24 in *Massachusetts v. KalshiEx*, arguing that the Commodity Exchange Act does not preempt state authority over sports betting and that Kalshi's sports contracts are gambling subject to state licensing under Massachusetts law. The brief was authored by Nevada AG Aaron Ford and Ohio AG Dave Yost, with signatories spanning red and blue states from Alabama and Alaska through Wisconsin, plus the District of Columbia. The news cycle developed across April 27 and 28. ([NY AG release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-joins-bipartisan-coalition-defending-states-gambling-laws) · [Arizona Capitol Times, April 27](https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/04/27/38-state-ags-sign-amicus-brief-supporting-mass-in-kalshi-lawsuit/) · [The Block, April 27](https://www.theblock.co/post/398886/cftc-sues-new-york-over-prediction-market-crackdown-as-38-ags-back-massachusetts-kalshi-case) · [Amicus brief PDF](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/amicus-curiae/massachusetts-v-kalshiex-llc-amicus-brief-2026.pdf))

The CFTC filed its own amicus brief on the same docket on the same day, asserting that the Commodity Exchange Act grants the agency exclusive jurisdiction over event contracts and that state gambling enforcement against CFTC-registered platforms is preempted. The agency is making the same statutory argument in active suits against Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, and, as of April 24, New York. ([bettorsinsider, April 28](https://bettorsinsider.com/sports-betting/2026/04/28/38-attorneys-general-just-lined-up-against-prediction-markets-while-the-cftc-takes-the-fight-to-the-massachusetts-supreme-court/))

The AGs' brief argued in its own language that Kalshi's preemption theory "threatens the states' longstanding ability to protect their citizens" and that Dodd-Frank "was designed to regulate financial products tied to the 2008 financial crisis," not to create a federal pathway for unregulated sports gambling. The brief also notes that Kalshi users wagered more than $1 billion every month on the platform in 2025, with sports betting accounting for roughly 90% of monthly volume in certain months. ([Amicus brief PDF](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/amicus-curiae/massachusetts-v-kalshiex-llc-amicus-brief-2026.pdf))

<ImplicationBox items={["A 38-state coalition with bipartisan span is the broadest formal state-side filing assembled in this preemption fight. It signals to the SJC, to other state courts, and to the Ninth Circuit that the state-side argument has institutional weight beyond the individual plaintiffs.", "The CFTC litigating on the same docket as 38 AGs compresses what would normally be a multi-circuit dispute into a single state court's preemption analysis. The downstream consequences extend to the parallel Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, New York, and Nevada cases.", "Whichever way the SJC rules, the losing side has a clear path to federal review. ## International

### India's PROGA takes effect, banning Kalshi and Polymarket

India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules (PROGA) took effect on May 1, formally banning "online money games" and sweeping prediction-market platforms into the prohibition. India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) had previously sent letters to VPN service providers warning that users were still accessing "illegal and blocked prediction market and online betting platforms." ([Bitcoin World](https://bitcoinworld.co.in/polymarket-kalshi-india-crackdown/) · [BloomingBit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/112305) · [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/prediction-market-giants-defy-india-ban-in-high-stakes-global-expansion-play/))

Kalshi and Polymarket continued accepting Indian users after the ban took effect. A single Indian Premier League match on May 7 (Lucknow Super Giants vs. Royal Challengers Bengaluru) drew more than $27 million in combined trading across the two platforms, according to Bloomberg. ([Bloomberg, May 18](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-18/kalshi-polymarket-defy-india-ban-on-online-betting-platforms))

## Company News

### Kalshi tops Polymarket on April volume

Kalshi reported $14.8 billion in April trading volume, a roughly 13% increase over March and an all-time monthly high for the platform; multiple trackers corroborate the figure. Polymarket's April volume declined, with reported figures varying between $9.0 billion (a 14.8% decline) and $10.3 billion (a 9% decline) depending on the source. Both totals are below Polymarket's March figure, and April is the first month Kalshi has surpassed Polymarket on monthly volume in eight months. Polymarket attributed part of the decline to an April 28 technical upgrade that briefly halted trading and cleared open orders; the platform's active traders fell from roughly 733,000 in March to about 643,000 in April. ## Industry Outlook

The two pending decisions that will define the rest of May are the Ninth Circuit's ruling on the Nevada sports-preemption appeal and the Senate Commerce hearing on May 20, where the platforms are expected to testify. The Senate's self-binding gives the platforms a useful posture going into that hearing, since every major platform endorsed it; insider-trading policy enters the conversation on terms the platforms helped set. 38 state attorneys general and the CFTC arguing opposite preemption theories on the same Massachusetts court docket compresses what would otherwise be a multi-circuit dispute into a single state-court analysis with national implications. And the CFTC's input phase closed with a comment record too broad and too cross-aligned to permit a clean federal preemption rule in any eventual NPRM.
