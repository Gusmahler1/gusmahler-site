## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC Chair defends exclusive jurisdiction before Congress

CFTC Chair Selig appeared before the House Agriculture Committee to defend the agency's exclusive jurisdiction over prediction markets. He characterized state enforcement actions as unconstitutional, reaffirmed the CFTC framework as the governing authority, and was direct on market integrity: *"I want to be crystal clear to anyone who engages in fraud, manipulation or insider trading in any of our markets: we will find you, and the full force of the law will come to bear."*

Committee members pressed Selig on whether ANPRM rulemaking would address the specific contract types targeted in pending congressional bills. No firm commitments emerged.

## Federal Courts

### Ninth Circuit panel signals skepticism toward platforms on preemption

A three-judge Ninth Circuit panel heard oral arguments in the consolidated Nevada Gaming Control Board case, which consolidates challenges from Kalshi, NADEX, and Robinhood Derivatives. The case turns on the same CEA preemption question the Third Circuit decided in platforms' favor last week. Judges Ryan Nelson, Bridget Bade, and Kenneth Lee presided; Judge Nelson appeared particularly skeptical of the platforms' arguments. A ruling is expected within 60 to 120 days.

A decision against platforms would create a direct circuit split. Circuit splits of this magnitude typically trigger Supreme Court review, with resolution likely extending into 2027.

## Market & Industry

### Bernstein projects $1 trillion in annual volume by 2030

Bernstein Research published a forecast projecting prediction market trading volume at $1 trillion annually by 2030 — roughly 80% compound annual growth from 2025 levels. The firm estimates 2026 volume at approximately $240 billion, a 370% increase year-over-year. Sports contracts are estimated to account for 60% or more of current volume. Regulatory and legislative uncertainty is identified as the primary downside risk to the projection.
