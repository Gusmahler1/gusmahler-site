## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### CFTC files amicus brief defending federal preemption of state gambling laws

On February 17, the CFTC filed an amicus brief in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit in *North American Derivatives Exchange, Inc. v. State of Nevada* — the first time the agency has formally entered court proceedings to defend the legal status of prediction market event contracts. The brief advances three interlocking legal arguments: (1) prediction market event contracts qualify as "swaps" under Section 1a(47) of the Commodity Exchange Act; (2) the CEA's regulatory framework field-preempts state gambling laws as applied to contracts listed on designated contract markets; and (3) state gambling laws are independently preempted under conflict preemption doctrine, because allowing state enforcement would undermine the uniform federal framework Congress established.

The CFTC's entry as an amicus — rather than waiting for cases to reach federal regulatory appeals — reflects Chairman Selig's public commitment to supporting the industry through litigation as well as rulemaking. The Nevada case is the lead vehicle for the federal preemption argument, and a favorable Ninth Circuit ruling would create circuit-level precedent applicable across the western United States. ([CFTC Press Release 9183-26, February 17](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9183-26)  ·  [CNBC, February 17](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/17/cftc-defends-prediction-market-enforcement-states-challenge.html)  ·  [CoinDesk, February 17](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/02/17/cftc-s-selig-opens-legal-dispute-against-states-getting-in-way-of-prediction-markets))

## State-Level Legislative Activity

### Utah Governor Cox commits to signing HB243, framing prediction markets as de facto casinos

Utah Governor Spencer Cox publicly confirmed he will sign HB243 (Gambling Revisions), a bill that would redefine "proposition betting" under state law to encompass event contract platforms. The bill passed the Utah House on February 10 and was headed to the Senate for a vote. Cox described platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket as "putting a casino in the pocket of every single American" — among the most prominent gubernatorial statements against the industry to date. Utah has prohibited gambling since statehood and HB243 is designed specifically to close the gap between the state's existing gambling prohibitions and the emergence of federally regulated event contract exchanges. ([Deseret News, February 17](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2026/02/17/governor-cox-vows-legal-battle-over-prediction-markets/)  ·  [KSL, February 17](https://www.ksl.com/article/51449767/gov-spencer-cox-vows-fight-to-keep-prediction-markets-out-of-utah))

<ImplicationBox items={["Utah's willingness to pass legislation specifically targeting federally regulated prediction markets — and the Governor's explicit intent to enforce it — tests the preemption doctrine in a way that cease-and-desist letters do not. If Utah enforces HB243 against a CFTC-registered DCM, it will create a direct statutory conflict with the CEA, which is exactly the kind of case that could produce a definitive federal ruling on preemption."]} />

## Company News

### Novig raises $75 million Series B at $500 million valuation

Novig announced the close of a $75 million Series B round on February 18, led by Pantera Capital, with participation from Multicoin Capital, Makers Fund, Edge Equity, and existing investors Forerunner Ventures, Perceptive Ventures, and NFX. The round values the company at $500 million. Novig has been operating under a sweepstakes gaming model — which allows it to offer free-to-play prediction gaming with a dual-currency system — while its DCM application (filed under the entity name Ludlow Exchange) is pending at the CFTC. The company has stated its intent to build a "trader-first sports prediction market" once DCM status is secured.
