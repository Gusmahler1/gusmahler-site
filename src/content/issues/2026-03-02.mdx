## Federal Regulation & CFTC

### Two coordinated CFTC actions on February 25: rule withdrawal and enforcement advisory

On February 25, the CFTC released two items that together define the agency's current position on prediction markets under Chairman Selig.

#### CFTC withdraws 2024 proposed event contracts rule and 2025 staff advisory

The CFTC formally withdrew its June 2024 proposed rulemaking, which would have broadly identified political and sports-related event contracts as "contrary to the public interest" and effectively barred them from CFTC-regulated exchanges. The agency also withdrew a 2025 staff advisory that had cautioned registrants about listing sports-related contracts pending ongoing litigation. The official rationale cited in the withdrawal: "various forms of state regulatory actions and litigation concerning the Commission's exclusive jurisdiction over event contract derivatives listed on designated contract markets." The withdrawal removes the prior administration's restrictive framework and signals that new rules — expected to take a permissive rather than prohibitive form — are coming through a fresh rulemaking process. ([CFTC Press Release 9179-26, February 25](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9179-26)  ·  [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/cftc-event-contracts-prediction-markets-support/)  ·  [Corporate Compliance Insights](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/cftc-withdraws-proposed-rule-prediction-markets/))

#### CFTC Division of Enforcement issues prediction markets insider trading advisory

In a separate release on February 25, the CFTC's Division of Enforcement published an advisory making explicit what had been implicit: CEA anti-manipulation and insider trading prohibitions apply directly to participants trading on prediction market contracts, including those involving politics, sports, and current events. The advisory was accompanied by the public disclosure of two enforcement cases previously handled by Kalshi's internal compliance team. In the first, a political candidate who traded on his own election results was fined $2,246 and suspended from Kalshi for five years. In the second, a YouTube channel editor who likely had advance access to unpublished video content traded contracts related to that channel and was fined $20,397 with a two-year suspension. The CFTC used these cases to signal it will pursue enforcement of violations regardless of whether they are first surfaced by platform compliance or by the agency's own surveillance. ([CFTC Press Release 9185-26, February 25](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9185-26)  ·  [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/the-cftc-advises-prediction-market-users-insider-information-is-not-fair-game/)  ·  [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/02/united-states-eye-on-enforcement))

## State-Level Activity

### Kalshi files preemptive federal lawsuit against Utah

On February 23, Kalshi filed a complaint in federal district court against Utah Governor Spencer Cox and other state officials, seeking a permanent injunction and declaratory relief to prevent Utah from enforcing its gambling laws against Kalshi's event contract platform. The lawsuit argues that Congress granted the CFTC "exclusive jurisdiction" over trading on CFTC-regulated exchanges, and that Utah's attempt to treat Kalshi's contracts as illegal proposition betting is preempted by federal law. The filing was made preemptively — before Utah had taken formal enforcement action — based on Cox's public statements of intent to sign HB243 and enforce it. This is Kalshi's first affirmative federal lawsuit against a state and represents a shift from purely defensive posture to proactive litigation strategy. ([KUTV](https://kutv.com/news/local/kalshi-files-lawsuit-against-utah-officials-escalating-fight-over-prediction-markets)  ·  [Gaming America](https://gamingamerica.com/news/utah-takes-on-kalshi-and-polymarket-in-landmark-prediction-market-fight))

<ImplicationBox items={["Filing before enforcement rather than after it is a deliberate legal strategy — preemptive suits allow the plaintiff to choose favorable venue and frame the legal question before the state can establish facts on the ground. ### Utah Senate approves HB243, bill heads to Governor

The Utah Senate passed HB243 (Gambling Revisions) on February 27, completing the legislature's action on the bill. The measure amends the state's gambling statute to explicitly define "proposition betting" in a way that captures event contract platforms — meaning that a CFTC-registered exchange offering sports or election contracts to Utah residents would be operating an illegal gambling business under state law. The bill, which passed the Utah House on February 10, had the Governor's already-stated commitment to sign. The bill's mechanism is straightforward: it does not attempt to create a licensing regime or regulate prediction markets — it treats them as categorically illegal gambling, the same enforcement framework used for unlicensed casino operations.
